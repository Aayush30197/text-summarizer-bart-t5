ACT I // SCENE 01: THE FOUNDATIONS

What is Abstractive Summarization?

Highlighter vs. Note-Taking

🖍️ Extractive Summarization (The Old Way)

Yellow Highlighter
"The algorithm selects existing sentences and copies them out verbatim."
  • How it works: Scores sentences by keyword frequency and copies the top 3 lines directly.
  • Advantage: Grammatically sound because human journalists wrote every word.
  • Fatal Flaw: Choppy transitions; cannot synthesize facts across paragraphs into new sentences.

✍️ Abstractive Summarization (Our Project)

Student Study Notes
"The AI reads the chapter, builds semantic meaning, and drafts new sentences from scratch."
  • How it works: Builds mathematical vectors of meaning, paraphrases, and writes new text.
  • Advantage: Produces human-like, flowing news synthesis that fuses multiple events.
  • The Grand Challenge: The model risks inventing facts or missing defining events.
🎯
The Capstone Goal: Take 1,000-word breaking news stories from CNN/DailyMail and produce accurate, publication-grade 3-sentence summaries in under a second using BART and T5.
ACT I // SCENE 02: THE VULNERABILITIES

Why is Abstractive Summarization Hard?

Probability Calculators vs. Objective Truth
👻

1. Extrinsic Hallucination

The AI predicts high-probability phrases and invents people, stadiums, or external headlines that never occurred in the source text.

Example: Injecting a Virginia teenager's name into a story taking place in South Africa.
✂️

2. Critical Fact Omission

The AI consumes its token budget on background bureaucratic details and completely forgets to report the actual criminal indictment.

Example: Describing firearms training records while omitting the manslaughter verdict.
🔁

3. Repetition Loops

Autoregressive decoders get trapped in mathematical feedback cycles, repeating words indefinitely without explicit penalties.

Example: "The police said the police said the police said..."
💡
The Core Dilemma: Language models do not possess an internal fact-checker. They generate the most probable next word, which makes them vulnerable to confident fabrications unless properly constrained!
ACT II // SCENE 01: THE CONTENDERS

Meet the Titans: BART vs. T5

Meta's Denoising Repairman vs. Google's Text-to-Text

🔵 BART: The Sentence Repairman

Meta AI (Lewis et al. 2020)

Denoising Autoencoder: Meta damaged text with random corruptions and trained BART to reconstruct the pristine original.

• Token Masking: Replacing words with [MASK]
• Sentence Permutation: Shuffling sentences out of order
• Document Rotation: Starting from a random middle sentence
✨ Why it excels: Naturally trained to reorder, prune, and reconstruct coherent language.

🟢 T5: The Universal Converter

Google Research (Raffel 2020)

Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer: Casts every NLP problem as plain text input $\to$ plain text output.

• Translation: translate English to German: ...
• Question Answering: question: ... context: ...
• Summarization: summarize: [article text]
✨ Why it excels: Unified multi-task learning across massive web-crawled corpora (C4).
🧠
Self-Attention in Plain English: In the sentence "The boy dropped the glass because he was careless", self-attention calculates a mathematical link connecting "he" back to "the boy" across distance!

📐 Why 512 Words? The Inverted Pyramid

Self-attention memory scales quadratically: O(N²). Doubling article length quadruples GPU RAM! In journalism, the Inverted Pyramid dictates placing the 5 W's (Who, What, Where, When, Why) at the very top. 512 tokens captures the headline facts while saving 4× compute.

ACT III // SCENE 02: THE GENERATION MECHANICS

Decoding Strategy: How Decoders Think

Greedy vs. Beam Search & Repetition Penalties

⚡ Greedy Search (num_beams = 1)

Picks the single highest-probability next token at each step (e.g., 60% 'The' vs 25% 'A').

  • Pro: Lightning fast inference.
  • Con: Short-sighted; gets trapped in repetitive dead-ends.

🔭 Beam Search (num_beams = 4 — OUR CHOICE)

Maintains 4 candidate sentences in parallel, pruning weak paths at every step.

  • Pro: Produces cohesive, globally optimal paragraphs.
  • Trade-off: ~3× more compute operations per token.
Decoding Parameter Fixed Value Specific Engineering Purpose
num_beams 4 Considers 4 alternative generation paths simultaneously
min_length / max_length 30 / 128 Guarantees complete 3–4 sentence news summaries
length_penalty 2.0 Encourages models to finish full thoughts without cutting off
no_repeat_ngram_size 3 Bans repeating any 3-word phrase $\to$ 0.0% loop rate!
ACT IV // SCENE 01: THE EVALUATION SYSTEM

Grading the AI: What is ROUGE?

Word-Matching vs. Factual Truth

ROUGE-1

Single-word overlap (unigrams)

Measures shared topical vocabulary and names.

ROUGE-2

Two-word phrase overlap (bigrams)

Measures phrasing flow (e.g. "White House").

ROUGE-L

Longest Common Subsequence

Measures grammatical sentence structure.

ROUGE-Lsum

Sentence-level Summary LCS

Splits summaries at newline boundaries.

🚨 The Fatal Blindspot: The "Voted YES vs. NO" Trap Catastrophic Error
Human Journalist:
"The senator voted NO on the tax bill."
AI Generated:
"The senator voted YES on the tax bill."
Word Overlap: 6 out of 7 words match! ROUGE-1 = 85.7% (A+) Factual Truth: 100% WRONG (Opposite meaning)

ACT V // SCENE 01: BEHIND THE CURTAIN

Engineering Challenges & The Three Bugs We Solved

Debugging Transformers on Real Hardware

💥 1. T5 fp16 NaN Loss at Step 42

Symptom: Loss exploded to NaN (Not a Number) at step 42 on the Kaggle T4 GPU.

Root Cause: T5 uses RMSNorm without additive bias. In half-precision fp16, numbers cannot exceed 65,504. Operation accumulation caused backward gradient overflow.

Fix: Enforced fp16: False (fp32) for T5 in configs.

💾 2. CUDA Out-Of-Memory

Symptom: Articles longer than 1,500 words caused GPU allocation crashes.

Root Cause: Full self-attention requires O(N²) memory. Long sequences exhaust GPU tensor buffers during backprop.

Fix: Enforced 512 token truncation via Inverted Pyramid.

🔁 3. Repetition Loops

Symptom: Early summaries repeated phrases (e.g. "in the city in the city").

Root Cause: Greedy/beam paths falling into local high-probability token cycles.

Fix: Set no_repeat_ngram_size = 3 $\to$ loop rate 0.0%.
🛠️
The Engineer's Lesson: You cannot treat transformers as identical black boxes. BART and T5 have completely different normalization layers and numerical tolerance limits!

FINALE // SCENE 01: THE DEFENSE

Oral Defense Mastery: Hardest Questions from Dr. Li

Technical Mastery & Research Defense

Q1: Why higher ROUGE for T5 but lower human factuality?

Answer: ROUGE measures n-gram overlap, not semantic truth. T5 generated longer summaries (100 tokens vs. BART's 83), giving it more chances to intersect with reference vocabulary. However, human evaluation showed BART captured events better (4.24 vs. 3.88) with fewer omissions and hallucinations.

Q2: Why did T5 produce NaN loss in fp16?

Answer: T5 relies on RMSNorm without additive bias. In IEEE 754 float16, the maximum finite value is 65,504. Backward operations caused overflow, generating NaN gradients at step 42. Setting fp16: False (fp32) restored numerical stability.

Q3: Why 512 tokens instead of 1,024?

Answer: Self-attention memory scales quadratically: O(N²). 1,024 tokens caused memory exhaustion on the GPU. Furthermore, journalism's Inverted Pyramid frontloads the 5 W's, making 512 tokens an optimal compute-accuracy tradeoff.

Architectural Decision Matrix

  • You prioritize factual grounding: Achieved higher human factuality (4.88 / 5.0) and fewer hallucinations.

  • You want a unified multi-task system: A single model handling translation, QA, and summarization via prefixes.
