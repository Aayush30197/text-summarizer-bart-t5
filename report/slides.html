Teaching Computers to Summarize News: BART vs. T5

🖍️ Extractive Summarization (Old Way)

Like using a highlighter in a textbook. It simply picks existing sentences and copies them out.

  • Easy to do, but sounds robotic and disjointed.
  • Cannot combine ideas or write new sentences.

✍️ Abstractive Summarization (What We Do)

Like taking notes in your own words. The AI reads the entire article, figures out what matters, and writes brand-new sentences.

  • Sounds fluent and natural, like a human journalist.
  • Much harder to get right because the AI can make mistakes.
💡 Real-World Goal: Turn a 1,000-word breaking news article from CNN into a clean 3-sentence summary in under half a second, without losing critical facts or making things up.

SECTION 3.1: TASK & MODEL INTRODUCTION (5 MIN) • SLIDE 2 OF 10

Why is Summarization So Hard for AI?

👻 1. "Hallucinations" (Making Stuff Up)

Because AI predicts words based on patterns, it might confidently invent a fake stadium name, misattribute a quote, or mention a completely different person who was never in the article!

✂️ 2. Missing the Main Point

A news article might be about a murder trial, but the AI gets distracted summarizing the police department's paperwork and forgets to mention whether the suspect was guilty or innocent!

🔁 3. Getting Stuck in Loops

Without clever math rules, an AI model can repeat: "The police said said said..." or rephrase the same thought four times in a row until it runs out of space.

🎯 What We Set Out to Answer:

  1. Which model writes summaries that match professional journalists better?
  2. Which model stays truthful and avoids inventing fake facts?
  3. Which model is fast enough to actually use in a live, real-time website?

Meet the Contenders: BART vs. T5

🔵 BART (Built by Meta / Facebook)

Size: ~140 Million Parameters • 6 Encoder & 6 Decoder Layers

The Sentence Repairman: BART was trained by taking clean articles, masking out words with blanks, scrambling sentences, and teaching the model to reconstruct the original article.

Analogy: Like giving someone a shredded, redacted newspaper and training them to piece the story back together. Great at condensing!

🟢 T5 (Built by Google)

Size: ~220 Million Parameters • 12 Encoder & 12 Decoder Layers

The Universal Text Converter: T5 treats every single problem in AI as a text-in, text-out task. To make it summarize, you literally have to tell it: summarize: [article].

Analogy: Like an all-in-one smart assistant that can answer questions, translate Spanish, or summarize—as long as you give it the right prompt.

📰 The "Inverted Pyramid" Strategy

Journalists write news with the most important facts right at the top. Even though some news stories are 2,000 words long, reading the first 512 words gives the AI almost everything it needs to summarize the main event.

⚖️ Strict Parity (No Favoritism)

Both models were given the exact same batch size (32), the same optimizer (AdamW), and generated summaries using the same beam search settings: exploring 4 paths and writing between 30 and 128 words.

How Do We Grade AI Summaries? (What is ROUGE?)

📏 ROUGE = Overlap with a Human Journalist

We compare what the AI wrote against what a real human editor wrote. The more key words and phrases they share, the higher the score.

ROUGE-1 (Single Words)

Counts matching single words.
e.g., "president", "election", "airport"

Measures general topic coverage.

ROUGE-2 (Pairs of Words)

Counts matching two-word phrases.
e.g., "Prime Minister", "gold medal"

Measures phrasing fidelity.

ROUGE-L (Sentence Flow)

Measures the longest shared sequence of words in sentence order.

Measures structural coherence.
⚠️ The Catch: ROUGE is dumb! If an AI writes "The president died" instead of "The president lived", it gets a 66% ROUGE score even though it reported the exact opposite of reality! That's why we also need human eyes.

SECTION 3.3: ERRORS & LIMITATIONS (5 MIN) • SLIDE 9 OF 10

Limitations: What Would We Do With More Compute?

1. Compute & Quota Constraints

We trained for 1 full epoch on 12,000 articles. While this gave us an amazing +12% quality boost, commercial models train on millions of articles across weeks of supercomputing.

2. Long-Form Investigative Articles

Cutting articles at 512 words works great for breaking news, but fails on 5,000-word investigative reports where the key takeaway is buried in the final paragraph.

3. Human Study Sample Size

I evaluated 25 articles with a strict double-blind system. An expanded study would involve multiple human raters to calculate inter-annotator agreement.

4. The Golden Rule of AI Summaries

AI summarization is great for scanning news or executive overviews, but you should never rely on an AI summary for critical legal, medical, or financial decisions without checking the source!

SECTION 3.5: Q&A & CONCLUSION (5 MIN) • SLIDE 10 OF 10

Summary: Which Model Should You Choose?

🏆 Pick BART-base If:

  • You need speed: Runs at 0.31s per article (almost 2× faster than T5).
  • You want direct facts: Scored higher on human news coverage (4.24 vs. 3.88) and factual accuracy.
  • You have a budget: Trained in just 11.5 minutes with half the energy and memory of T5.

🏆 Pick T5-base If:

  • You want higher word overlap: Led by +2.65% ROUGE-1 (42.88%).
  • You want longer, flowing prose: Averages 100 words with compound sentence fluency.
  • You want one AI for multiple tasks: T5 can do translation, QA, and summarization in one model.
