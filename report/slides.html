Comparative Evaluation of BART and T5 for Abstractive Summarization

🖍️ Extractive Summarization (Traditional)

Like highlighting lines in a textbook. The algorithm ranks existing sentences and copies them out verbatim.

  • Guaranteed grammatically sound, but choppy and disjointed.
  • Cannot combine facts, paraphrase, or generate new phrasing.

✍️ Abstractive Summarization (Our Focus)

Like synthesizing notes in your own words. The AI reads the entire document, builds semantic representations, and drafts new sentences.

  • Produces fluent, human-like journalistic summaries.
  • High-stakes challenge: models risk inventing fake facts ('hallucinating').
🎯 Core Capstone Objective: Establish a strictly controlled, reproducible experimental pipeline that fine-tunes and evaluates BART-base (~140M) and T5-base (~220M) side-by-side across automated ROUGE metrics, latency/memory efficiency, and double-blind human factuality.

Why is Summarization Hard? Four Core Research Questions

👻 1. Factual Hallucination

The AI predicts high-probability phrases and invents fake stadium names, wrongful suspects, or external news headlines not in the source text.

✂️ 2. Critical Fact Omission

The AI wastes its limited token budget on introductory context and cuts off before reporting the criminal charge or game resolution.

🔁 3. Repetition Loops

Decoders can cycle into degenerate phrase stuttering unless constrained by strict n-gram blocking during beam exploration.

🎯 RQ1 & RQ2: Quality & Truthfulness

  • RQ1 (Quality): Which architecture achieves higher lexical n-gram recall (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L) on 1,000 test articles?
  • RQ2 (Factuality): How faithfully do generated summaries preserve core facts versus introducing hallucinations in blinded human scoring?

⚙️ RQ3 & RQ4: Decoding & Efficiency

  • RQ3 (Decoding): How do beam search constraints (num_beams=4, length penalty=2.0, 3-gram blocking) eliminate repetition loops?
  • RQ4 (Efficiency): What are the real-world trade-offs in training duration, inference latency (seconds/article), and memory footprint?

Automated Evaluation: Demystifying ROUGE Metrics

ROUGE-1 (Unigram Overlap)

Counts individual matching words between the AI summary and the human reference.

Measures: Broad factual entity and topical coverage.

ROUGE-2 (Bigram Overlap)

Counts matching consecutive 2-word pairs.
e.g., 'Prime Minister', 'manslaughter charge'

Measures: Phrasing fidelity and idiomatic accuracy.

ROUGE-L (Longest Common Subsequence)

Measures the longest shared sequence of words that appear in the exact same sentence order.

Measures: Grammatical flow and sentence structure.

⚠️ The Fundamental Flaw of ROUGE: Blindness to Factual Truth

If an AI generates: "The senator voted YES on the bill" when the human truth was "The senator voted NO on the bill", ROUGE scores this at 85.7% accuracy despite stating the exact opposite of reality! ROUGE rewards lexical mimicry, not truthfulness.

SECTION 3.3: ERRORS & DEBUGGING CHALLENGES • SLIDE 8 OF 12

Engineering Challenges, Debugging & Solutions

🐛 Challenge 1: The T5 fp16 NaN Loss Bug

The Problem: When fine-tuning T5 on Kaggle T4 GPUs in mixed precision (fp16), the loss crashed to NaN at step 42!

Root Cause: T5 lacks additive biases and uses Root Mean Square LayerNorm, causing gradient underflow/overflow in 16-bit floats.

Fix: Enforced fp32 execution for T5 while training BART stably in fp16.

✂️ Challenge 2: Long Document Truncation

The Problem: CNN articles often exceed 1,500 words, but quadratic attention memory explodes on student GPU quotas.

Architectural Trade-off: Setting max_source_length=512 leverages the journalistic 'inverted pyramid'.

Result: Preserves 90%+ of core lead facts while cutting GPU memory by 60%.

🔁 Challenge 3: Eliminating Repetition

The Problem: Beam search decoders frequently cycle into repetitive phrasing (e.g. 'police said the police said').

Algorithmic Fix: Enforced no_repeat_ngram_size = 3 and length penalty of 2.0 in configs/decoding.yaml.

Result: Achieved exactly 0 repetition loops across all 1,000 test articles.

Final Synthesis: When to Choose BART vs. T5

🏆 Pick BART-base If:

  • You need speed & responsiveness: 0.309s per article (1.84× faster inference).
  • You need high factual accuracy: 4.88 / 5.0 factual grounding; captures lead news events.
  • You have a tight compute budget: Trains in 11.5 minutes with stable fp16 mixed precision.

🏆 Pick T5-base If:

  • You want maximum n-gram recall: Leads by +2.65% ROUGE-1 (42.88%) and +2.53% ROUGE-2.
  • You want compound sentence flow: Averages 100 tokens with finished sentence boundaries.
  • You want a multi-task pipeline: Shared model for translation, QA, and summarization.
