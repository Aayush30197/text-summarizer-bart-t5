MODULE 1: FOUNDATIONS • WHAT ARE WE DOING AND WHY?

NLP, Text Summarization, and the Abstractive Challenge

🖍️ Extractive Summarization (Yellow Highlighter)

The algorithm scores existing sentences and copies the highest-scoring sentences directly from the original article.

  • Advantages: Grammatically sound (human-written), computationally fast, never invents new facts.
  • Disadvantages: Choppy transitions, repetitive phrasing, cannot synthesize ideas across paragraphs into a single sentence.

✍️ Abstractive Summarization (Student Notes — OUR PROJECT)

The AI reads the article, constructs an internal semantic representation, and generates brand-new sentences in its own words.

  • Advantages: Paraphrases complex stories, fuses multiple facts smoothly, produces natural human-like news summaries.
  • Disadvantages: Risk of hallucinations, omitting key facts, or generating unsupported statements.

⚠️ Three Major Vulnerabilities in Abstractive Summarization

1. Extrinsic Hallucination

The model invents information that never appeared in the source article (e.g., generating a Virginia teenager's name into a South African story).

2. Critical Fact Omission

The model uses its length budget on background details and completely omits the defining event or criminal indictment.

3. Repetition Loops

The autoregressive decoder falls into a loop repeating the same phrase (e.g., "the police said the police said...").

MODULE 2: THE TWO MODELS • ARCHITECTURAL COMPARISON

Transformer Foundations: BART-base vs. T5-base

🔵 Model 1: BART-base (Meta AI, 2020)

Denoising Autoencoder: Pretrained by intentionally corrupting text and learning to reconstruct the original uncorrupted document.

  • Corruptions: Token masking ([MASK]), deletion, sentence shuffling, document rotation.
  • Input Format: Plain natural text (no prefix required).
  • Training Precision: fp16 mixed precision.

🟢 Model 2: T5-base (Google Research, 2020)

Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer: Frames every NLP problem as text-in, text-out using a unified task-conditioning prefix.

  • Pretraining: C4 dataset with masked span corruption & supervised tasks.
  • Input Format: Explicit task prefix: summarize: [article text].
  • Training Precision: fp32 full precision (due to RMSNorm).

MODULE 4: DECODING STRATEGY • GENERATION MECHANICS

Greedy Search vs. Beam Search & N-Gram Blocking

⚡ Greedy Search (num_beams = 1)

At each step, greedily selects the single highest-probability next token (e.g., 60% 'The' vs. 25% 'A').

  • Pro: Fast inference speed, minimal compute overhead.
  • Con: Short-sighted; locally optimal choices lead to globally unnatural sentences and repetition loops.

🔭 Beam Search (num_beams = 4 — OUR CHOICE)

Maintains the 4 most promising sequence hypotheses simultaneously, expanding and pruning paths at each token step.

  • Pro: Produces globally cohesive, high-quality multi-sentence summaries.
  • Trade-off: ~3× higher computational latency than greedy decoding.
Decoding Parameter Fixed Value Specific Engineering Purpose
num_beams 4 Explores 4 candidate hypotheses in parallel
min_length / max_length 30 / 128 Enforces substantial, well-formed 3–4 sentence news summaries
length_penalty 2.0 Favors longer, complete thoughts over premature termination
no_repeat_ngram_size 3 Blocks repetitive 3-word loops (e.g., "in the city in the city")
early_stopping True Terminates beam expansion as soon as 4 complete sequences are found
🛡️ Impact of 3-Gram Blocking: Across all 1,000 test articles evaluated, setting no_repeat_ngram_size = 3 achieved an exact 0.0% repetition loop rate for both BART and T5!
MODULE 5: EVALUATION METRICS • UNDERSTANDING ROUGE

ROUGE Metric Family & The Fatal 'Voted YES/NO' Blindspot

ROUGE-1 (Unigrams)

Single-word lexical overlap.

Measures shared topical vocabulary and named entities.

ROUGE-2 (Bigrams)

Two consecutive word phrases.

Measures phrasing flow (e.g., "criminal charge").

ROUGE-L (LCS)

Longest Common Subsequence.

Measures grammatical sentence-level structure.

ROUGE-Lsum

Sentence-level summary LCS.

Splits multi-sentence summaries at newlines.

🚨 The Fatal Flaw of Automated ROUGE: The "Voted YES vs. NO" Trap

Human Ground-Truth Reference:
"The senator voted NO on the tax bill."
AI-Generated Summary:
"The senator voted YES on the tax bill."
Word Overlap: 6 out of 7 words match! ROUGE-1 Score = 85.7% (Near-Perfect!)
Actual Factuality: 100% FALSE (Catastrophic Error)
🎓 The Lesson: A high ROUGE score does not mean a summary is true! This is why our project paired automated ROUGE evaluation with a rigorous double-blind human evaluation and error taxonomy.

MODULE 8: ENGINEERING CHALLENGES • DEBUGGING & LESSONS LEARNED

Three Critical Engineering Bugs and How We Solved Them

💥 1. T5 fp16 NaN Loss at Step 42

Symptom: Training loss collapsed to NaN (Not a Number) at step 42 on the T4 GPU.

Root Cause: T5 uses RMSNorm without additive bias. In fp16, numbers cannot exceed 65,504. Mathematical operations caused overflow in backward gradients.

Fix: Explicitly set fp16: False (fp32) for T5 in configs/t5.yaml.

💾 2. CUDA Out-Of-Memory (OOM)

Symptom: Articles longer than 1,500 words crashed PyTorch with CUDA OOM during backprop.

Root Cause: Full self-attention requires O(N²) memory. Long sequences exhaust GPU memory tensors.

Fix: Capped inputs at 512 tokens using journalism's Inverted Pyramid principle.

🔁 3. Repetition Loops in Generation

Symptom: Early summaries repeated phrases (e.g., "The police said the police said...").

Root Cause: Autoregressive decoders get trapped in local high-probability token cycles.

Fix: Enforced no_repeat_ngram_size = 3 in decoding config. Loop rate dropped to 0.0%.
🎓 Core Engineering Takeaway: You cannot treat transformers as interchangeable black boxes. Different architectures (LayerNorm in BART vs. RMSNorm in T5) have fundamentally different numerical precision and hardware stability requirements!

MODULE 11: PRESENTATION DEFENSE • TOUGHEST QUESTIONS FROM DR. LI

Oral Defense Preparation: Technical Q&A Mastery

Q1: Why higher ROUGE for T5 but lower human factuality?

Answer: ROUGE measures n-gram overlap, not semantic truth. T5 generated longer summaries (100 tokens vs. BART's 83), giving it more opportunities to intersect with reference vocabulary. However, human evaluation showed BART captured events better (4.24 vs. 3.88) with fewer omissions and hallucinations.

Q2: Why did T5 produce NaN loss in fp16?

Answer: T5 relies on RMSNorm without additive bias. In IEEE 754 float16, the maximum value is 65,504. Mathematical operations caused overflow, producing NaN gradients at step 42. Setting fp16: False (fp32) resolved the instability.

Q3: Why 512 tokens instead of 1,024?

Answer: Self-attention memory scales quadratically: O(N²). Doubling sequence length quadruples attention tensor allocations. Furthermore, journalistic Inverted Pyramid structure frontloads the 5 W's, making 512 tokens an optimal compute-information tradeoff.

Q4: What if you are asked something you don't know?

What to say: "That is a great question, Dr. Li. In our experiment, we evaluated on the 1,000-article test set under our fixed decoding config. We did not test that specific condition, but based on transformer theory, my hypothesis would be [...]. That would be an exciting direction for future work."

🎯 Golden Rule of Oral Defense: Never guess or invent an answer. Acknowledge the experimental boundary, state your hypothesis based on transformer fundamentals, and propose it as future work.
MODULE 12: ARCHITECTURAL DECISION MATRIX • FINAL CONCLUSION

When to Deploy BART vs. T5 & The Five Core Takeaways

🏆 Deploy BART-base If:

  • You need high throughput & low latency: Generates summaries in 0.309s (1.84× faster than T5).
  • You prioritize factual accuracy: Achieved higher human grounding (4.88 / 5.0) and fewer hallucinations.
  • You want concise summaries: Averages 83.1 tokens, getting straight to the core news facts.
  • You have limited GPU budget: Trains in half the time (11m 30s) using fp16 Tensor Cores.

🏆 Deploy T5-base If:

  • You optimize for ROUGE overlap: Leads with 42.88% ROUGE-1 and 20.24% ROUGE-2.
  • You want longer, flowing summaries: Averages 100.3 tokens with higher human fluency (4.40 / 5.0).
  • You need a multi-task system: Same model can handle translation, QA, and summarization via prefixing.
